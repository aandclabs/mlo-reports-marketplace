# MLO rules: what counts, what to skip, what to ask

A working summary of the LA Municipal Lobbying Ordinance (MLO, LAMC § 48.01 and following) and the Ethics Commission's guidance, for sorting calendar events and emails into lobbying, excluded and ask. It is not legal advice. Section 5 lists what is still uncertain. Section 6 lists the sources.

Contents: 1. Definitions (with the agency short names). 2. Counts as lobbying. 3. Exclude. 4. Ask (gray areas). 5. Uncertainties. 6. Sources.

## 1. Definitions

- **Lobbyist.** An individual who, in any consecutive three-month period, (1) has at least one direct communication with a City official or employee to try to influence City action, and (2) is compensated for 30 or more hours of lobbying activities.
- **Lobbying firm.** An entity (possibly a sole proprietor) that receives, or is entitled to receive, $1,000 or more in a consecutive three-month period for lobbying on behalf of others, and that has a partner, owner, shareholder, officer or employee who qualifies as a lobbyist. Assume the user's firm is one: this report is its Form 34.
- **Client.** The person who pays the firm, or on whose behalf the firm lobbies. The firm must register a client once that client pays or owes $250 or more in a calendar quarter for lobbying. This is the **$250 per quarter registration threshold**. A client below it may not belong in the report, so confirm with the user.
- **City official.** Any elected or appointed City officer, member, employee or consultant (who is a public official under the Political Reform Act) of any agency who takes part in considering municipal legislation, other than in a purely clerical, secretarial or ministerial capacity. The person does not have to be elected or council staff: any City employee counts. An `@lacity.org` address is a strong sign but not proof. Judge from the display name, email domain, calendar invite details and earlier contacts. If those don't settle it, ask.
- **Agency.** The City or any department, bureau, office, board or commission, including CRA/LA and HACLA. See the table below for the short names to use.
- **Direct communication.** Appearing as a witness before, talking to (by phone or in person), corresponding with, or answering questions or inquiries from, any City official or employee, personally or through an agent. Only direct communications become contacts in the report, with one exception: a neighborhood council meeting the user confirms as lobbying (section 4) is also recorded as a contact, with the council's name as `agency`. The council is never added to a matter's `agencies`, which lists City departments only.
- **Lobbying activities.** Compensated conduct related to a direct communication to influence municipal legislation: the direct communication itself, drafting ordinances, resolutions or regulations, advising a client on strategy, research and information gathering, influencing a third party (community, public or press relations) and, per the Ethics Commission, attending or monitoring City meetings and drafting planning applications. These count toward the 30 hours, but only direct communications are logged as contacts (apart from the neighborhood council exception above).
- **Municipal legislation.** Any legislative or administrative matter before an agency, including granting, denying, revoking, restricting or modifying a license, permit or entitlement for use (including all land use permits) when an official is charged with a final decision. It does **not** include:
  1. a request for advice or an interpretation of laws, regulations or approvals;
  2. any ministerial action, meaning one that does not require the official to use discretion about the outcome;
  3. collective-bargaining matters;
  4. preparing or compiling radius maps, vicinity maps, plot plans, site plans, owner or tenant lists, photos, proof of ownership or neighbor signatures for City Planning.

### Agencies and the short names to use

Use the short name in a contact's `agency` (the one official's agency) and in a matter's `agencies` (the departments lobbied on that matter). The second column lists other ways the agency appears in mail and invites, which are also the terms to search Gmail for. The one exception to using these names is a neighborhood council contact (see Direct communication above): its `agency` is the council's name, and the council never goes in `agencies`.

| Short name | Also appears as |
|---|---|
| Mayor's Office | Office of the Mayor, Mayor's staff |
| Council District N (N is 1 to 15, for example "Council District 13"), for the councilmember and the council office staff | Council District, CD13, councilmember's office, council office |
| City Planning | Department of City Planning, Planning |
| City Planning Commission | CPC |
| Area Planning Commission | APC. Add the area when known, for example "West Los Angeles Area Planning Commission" |
| LADBS | Building and Safety, Department of Building and Safety |
| LADOT | Department of Transportation, Transportation |
| Bureau of Engineering | BOE |
| LADWP | Water and Power, DWP |
| LAFD | Fire Department (plan check) |
| Cultural Heritage Commission | CHC |
| CRA/LA | CRA/LA |
| HACLA | Housing Authority of the City of Los Angeles |

Any other City department, bureau, office, board or commission is also an agency. Use its common name.

## 2. Counts as lobbying

A communication counts when the firm, working for a client, deals with a City official or employee about a discretionary land-use decision. The Ethics Commission's land-use guidance gives these examples:

- Getting a client's project **expedited**.
- Seeking to **remove conditions** from a conditional use permit (CUP).
- **Trying to change** an interpretation that City Planning has already given. Merely asking for the interpretation does not count (see section 3).
- Dealing with the City on any land use permit or entitlement where an official makes the final decision.

Also:

- Land-use professionals are covered: applicants for entitlements, property owners, permit expediters, architects and engineers.
- The official does not have to be elected or council staff. Any City employee counts.
- It counts in person, by phone, by email or letter, and by appearing at a hearing.

## 3. Exclude

Record each of these in `excluded` with its reason label (see "Excluded `reason`" in `references/form-34.md`).

| Reason label | Exclude when | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ministerial action | The contact was about an action with a fixed outcome that needs no discretion from the City employee, such as paying a fee or picking up a permit that is already approved. | The MLO leaves ministerial actions out of "municipal legislation". If you cannot tell whether the outcome was discretionary, it is an ask (section 4). |
| Interpretation request | The user only asked the City for advice or an interpretation of a law, regulation or approval, without trying to change the answer. | Requests for advice or an interpretation are excluded. Trying to change an interpretation counts as lobbying (section 2). |
| Internal or vendor meeting | No City official took part: the firm's own staff, a call with only the client, a vendor or a consultant. | A direct communication needs a City official or employee. |
| Non-City agency | The official works for an agency outside the City, such as LA County, Caltrans, the California Coastal Commission, or another county, state, federal or regional agency. | The MLO covers City agencies only. |
| Map or plot-plan preparation | The work was preparing or compiling radius maps, vicinity maps, plot plans, site plans, owner or tenant lists, photos, proof of ownership or neighbor signatures for City Planning. | The MLO leaves this work out of "municipal legislation". |

Skip, without recording, anything that has no link to the City, a client or a permit: personal events, unrelated meetings, and one-way mass mailings or automated notices. `excluded` is an audit trail of items that could have been lobbying, not a log of the whole calendar.

## 4. Ask (gray areas)

Do not decide these yourself. Put each in the numbered list in step 6 of the skill, with a suggested answer the user can accept, except where a row says to ask a question instead.

| Gray area | Why it is gray | Suggested answer to offer |
|---|---|---|
| LADBS "by right" or plan-check items | The MLO excludes ministerial actions, but the Ethics Commission warns that "by right" permits at LADBS can involve lobbying. It has to be decided case by case. | Not lobbying (reason: Ministerial action) for a routine status check, a correction or a fee question with a fixed outcome. Lobbying if the user asked the City to use judgment: expedite, grant an exception, change an interpretation or remove a condition. |
| Neighborhood council meetings | The Commission treats talking with neighborhood councils about a client's project as lobbying activity, because it seeks to influence a third party. That is not the same as a direct communication with a City official, so whether to log it as a contact is the user's call. | Lobbying. If the user agrees, record a contact with the council's name as `agency`. This is the one exception to "only direct communications become contacts" (section 1). Never add the council to the matter's `agencies`, which lists City departments only. |
| Unclear client | The item looks like lobbying but nothing ties it to a client: no matching name, address or case number. | Don't suggest a client. Ask "which client is this for?". Offer a client only when the event or email itself shows it (the client's name, or an address or case number that matches a matter you already have), and say what shows it. |
| Unclear official or agency | You cannot tell whether the person is a City official or employee (a personal email address, no title), or which department they work for. | Your best reading from the display name, email domain, calendar invite details or earlier contacts. If those don't settle it, ask. |

An unknown client or matter is also an ask (step 5 of the skill). Don't offer a guessed name for it: use only what the event or email shows, or ask.

## 5. Uncertainties

These are the open points. Describe each as uncertain (or, for Form 33, as not covered), never as settled law, and only when its "Mention it when" line applies. To resolve one, the user can contact the Ethics Commission ((213) 978-1960, ethics.commission@lacity.org) or counsel.

### Pending overhaul of the MLO

Council File 22-0560 would replace the 30-hour lobbyist test with a compensation threshold, clarify nonprofit and business improvement district registration, require lobbyists to identify themselves and their clients in public comment and written communications, and ban bundling. As of the City Clerk record checked on Oct 6, 2026, it is still pending in committee (last action Oct 21, 2025, continued to a date to be determined; the file expires Oct 21, 2027), and the Commission still describes the 30-hour test. If the user says it has since changed, don't argue; suggest checking the Clerk record. If it passes, who qualifies as a lobbyist changes, but the report's contents probably do not. A user's mention of "new ethics laws" may refer to this reform or to the Commission's land-use outreach, so don't assume which.

Mention it when: the user asks about new or changing lobbying rules, or about who has to register.

### LEFS field layout

Form 34 is filed only through the Commission's Lobbying Electronic Filing System (LEFS). No blank form or field list was publicly available, so the content shape follows LAMC § 48.08(C) item by item. The actual LEFS screens may be laid out differently.

Mention it when: the user asks how to enter the report in LEFS, or says a field does not match.

### How LEFS captures "departments lobbied"

The Commission says firm reports cover the departments lobbied, but § 48.08(C) spells out agencies only for individual contract lobbyists (C14). Commission regulations (C16) probably require them for firms. The report records agencies per matter either way.

Mention it when: the user asks why agencies are listed, or whether they are required.

### Ministerial or discretionary LADBS and plan-check work

The MLO excludes ministerial actions, but the Commission warns that "by right" permits can involve lobbying. The answer depends on the facts of each item, so treat these as ambiguous and ask.

Mention it when: an LADBS or plan-check item is in your questions. Say in one line that this is why you are asking.

### Whether the firm is a lobbying firm and each person a lobbyist

It depends on hours and compensation that Calendar and Gmail do not show. This skill assumes the user's firm is a registered lobbying firm and takes the lobbyists from the user's last report.

Mention it when: someone who is not on the lobbyists list has City contacts, or the user asks whether a person has to register. Do not tell the user who must register.

### Form 33

Each individual lobbyist a firm employs also files a Lobbyist Quarterly Report (Form 33). This skill prepares only the firm's Form 34 and does not cover Form 33.

Mention it when: the user asks what else has to be filed, or asks about individual lobbyist reports.
